# Warranty terminology

## Warranty and coverage

A manufacturer warranty is the manufacturer's promise to repair or replace a product that fails
because of a defect in materials or workmanship during a stated period. Coverage is the set of
failures the warranty pays for; anything outside coverage is either excluded or simply not
promised. A warranty claim asks the manufacturer to apply its warranty to one product.

## Warranty period

The warranty period runs from the purchase date on the proof of purchase for a stated number of
months. Some components, most often batteries, have their own shorter or longer period. A failure
is judged against the period of the component that failed. Periods can differ by region.

## Manufacturing defect

A manufacturing defect is a failure that originates in how the product was designed, built or
assembled, rather than in how it was used. Typical signs are failures under normal use without
visible external damage: the device does not power on, does not charge, a display or input stops
working, or a component fails intermittently.

## Exclusion

An exclusion is a cause of damage the warranty does not cover even inside the warranty period, such
as accidental damage, liquid damage, cosmetic wear or damage after an unauthorized repair. An
exclusion applies only when the evidence shows that excluded cause.

## Accidental damage

Accidental damage results from a sudden external event such as a drop, impact or crush: cracked or
shattered glass, dented or bent housings, broken ports. Some warranties exclude it entirely; others
cover a limited number of incidents within a window.

## Liquid damage

Liquid damage results from spills, submersion or moisture. Signs include corrosion on contacts,
triggered liquid-contact indicators, water marks under the display and residue inside ports.

## Proof of purchase

The proof of purchase is the invoice or receipt that shows the seller, the purchase date, the
product model and, ideally, the serial number and price. It establishes the start of the warranty
period and the region of purchase.

## Serial number

The serial number identifies one physical unit. It appears on a label or engraving on the product
and should match the serial number given in the claim and, when printed, on the invoice.

## Remedy

The remedy is what the manufacturer does for a covered claim: repair at an authorized service
center, replacement with an equivalent product, or, where policy allows, a refund.
