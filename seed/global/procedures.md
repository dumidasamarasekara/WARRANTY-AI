# General claim-handling procedures

## Completeness before judgement

A claim is judged only when it is complete: product model, serial number, purchase date, place of
purchase, a problem description, a legible proof of purchase and at least one photo of the product
and the problem. When something is missing or illegible, the submitter is asked for exactly the
missing items before any judgement is made.

## Judging coverage

Coverage is judged against the policy version in force on the purchase date, for the region of
purchase. The decisive questions, in order: is the product within the warranty period of the
failed component; does the evidence show an excluded cause; does the failure match a covered
defect. Every conclusion cites the policy clause it rests on.

## Weighing evidence

The invoice establishes the purchase; the photos establish the condition of the unit; the
description explains the failure. Where these sources disagree on a material fact — model, serial
number, seller, date or price — the disagreement is recorded and the claim goes to a human
reviewer.

## When to involve a reviewer

A claim goes to a human reviewer when its value exceeds the automatic limit, when its product
category always requires review, when any risk signal is present, when the analysis is uncertain
or incomplete, or when the automated analysis could not be completed.

## Explaining the outcome

Claimants receive a short, plain-language explanation of the outcome and the next step. It never
mentions internal risk assessments, fraud indicators or internal reference identifiers.
